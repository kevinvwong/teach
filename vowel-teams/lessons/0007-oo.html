The Two Sounds of oo

Why does oo make different sounds? The answer is a little tricky. There's no hard-and-fast rule. Instead, words that rhyme tend to share the same sound. You'll learn them in word families.

Long oo — /ū/

Like in moon. This is the more common sound.

moon   food   spoon   soon   zoo   room

Short oo — /ŭ/

Like in book. Used in fewer words.

book   look   good   foot   cook   wool

Tip: The short /ŭ/ sound of oo often appears in words that end with ook (book, look, cook, took, shook) or ood (good, wood, hood, stood). If you learn the families, you'll know the sound!

Quick Check

  1. Does "moon" use the long or short oo sound? Long
  2. Does "look" use the long or short oo sound? Short
  3. True or false: The word "good" rhymes with "mood." False — good uses short /ŭ/, mood uses long /ū/
