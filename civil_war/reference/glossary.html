Glossary

West Point & Civil War Generals

Key terms, classes, and personalities referenced throughout these lessons.

West Point & Military Terms

West Point
United States Military Academy (USMA) at West Point, New York. Founded 1802. Primary source of trained officers for both Union and Confederate armies.
Plebe
A first-year cadet at West Point.
Class of 1846
The most famous antebellum class. 59 graduates, 22 became generals (12 Union, 10 Confederate). Included Stonewall Jackson, George McClellan, A.P. Hill, George Pickett. Graduated as the Mexican-American War began.
Class of 1843
Grant's class. Produced 20 generals. Less famous individually but included Grant himself and Simon Bolivar Buckner.
Class of 1829
Robert E. Lee's class. Lee graduated 2nd (and without a single demerit). Also included Joseph E. Johnston.
Brevet
An honorary promotion awarded for gallantry, often with a higher rank title but lower pay.
Old Army
The small, pre-war regular U.S. Army. Officers knew each other well — they served together in Mexico, on the frontier, and in the Seminole Wars. This intimacy made the Civil War's divisions deeply personal.
Topographical Engineers
The elite corps of the Old Army — only the top West Point graduates could join. McClellan and Lee served in this corps.

Key Personalities

Robert E. Lee
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Class of 1829 (2nd). Commander of the Army of Northern Virginia. Perfectionist — never received a demerit at West Point. Nickname: "Marble Model."
AKA: "Marse Robert," "The Marble Model"
Ulysses S. Grant
Union: Yes. Confederate: No. Class of 1843 (21st of 39). General-in-Chief of Union armies. Unremarkable at West Point; saw himself as reluctantly there. Nickname: "Unconditional Surrender" Grant (from his initials USG).
AKA: "Sam" (at West Point), "Unconditional Surrender" Grant
Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Class of 1846 (17th of 59). Lee's most trusted lieutenant. Eccentric, fiercely religious. Struggled academically at first but grinded his way up the ranks.
AKA: "Stonewall," "Old Jack," "Tom Fool" (at West Point)
George B. McClellan
Union: Yes. Confederate: No. Class of 1846 (2nd of 59). Young Napoleon. Brilliant organizer, reluctant fighter. Commander of the Army of the Potomac. Dated Ellen Marcy (who A.P. Hill also loved).
AKA: "Little Mac," "The Young Napoleon"
James Longstreet
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Class of 1842 (54th of 56). Lee's "Old War Horse." Grant's close friend from pre-war days. Post-war became Grant's ally — marked as a "scalawag" by other Southerners.
AKA: "Old Pete," "Lee's Old War Horse"
George Pickett
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Class of 1846 (59th of 59 — last). Known for Pickett's Charge at Gettysburg. Popular, mischievous — played hooky at the local bar. Last in his class but became a major general.
AKA: None widely known
A.P. (Ambrose Powell) Hill
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Class of 1846 (15th of 59). McClellan's roommate and friend — until they both fell for Ellen Marcy. Commanded the "Light Division." Killed at Petersburg a week before the war ended.
AKA: "Little Powell"
Winfield Scott Hancock
Union: Yes. Confederate: No. Class of 1844. Armistead's best friend. Commanded the Union II Corps at Gettysburg — Armistead led the charge against him. Nickname: "Hancock the Superb."
AKA: "Hancock the Superb"
Lewis Armistead
Union: No. Confederate: Yes. Attended West Point but did not graduate (expelled after breaking a plate over another cadet's head). Hancock's best friend. Mortally wounded at Gettysburg during Pickett's Charge.
AKA: None widely known
George Armstrong Custer
Union: Yes. Confederate: No. Class of 1861 (last in his class). Reckless, flamboyant. Became the youngest Union general at 23. Captured his West Point classmate James Washington during the war.
AKA: "Autie," "Curly," "Fanny" (at West Point), "Boy General"
Jefferson Davis
Class of 1828. President of the Confederacy. Was at West Point with Lee and Albert Sidney Johnston. Feuded bitterly with Joseph E. Johnston (possibly over a woman, possibly over politics).

Crucial Events

Mexican-American War (1846–1848)
The "training ground" for the Civil War. Future Union and Confederate generals fought side by side under Zachary Taylor and Winfield Scott. They learned each other's strengths and weaknesses — knowledge they'd use against each other 15 years later.
Fort Sumter (April 1861)
The first battle of the Civil War. Orchestrated by P.G.T. Beauregard (Class of 1838) against his former West Point artillery instructor, Major Robert Anderson (Class of 1825). A West Pointer firing on another West Pointer: the war's first shot perfectly encapsulated its tragedy.
Gettysburg (July 1863)
The war's most famous battle. Site of Armistead's charge against Hancock, Pickett's disastrous assault, and dozens of personal reunions turned lethal.
Appomattox (April 1865)
Lee's surrender to Grant. The final class reunion. Grant's generosity in defeat (letting Confederates keep their horses, issuing parole passes) reflected the West Point code: fight hard, reconcile afterward.
