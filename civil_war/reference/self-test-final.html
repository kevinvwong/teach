Section 2: Who Am I?

  1. Ulysses S. Grant (born Hiram Ulysses Grant; the "S." came from a congressional appointment paperwork error; "Sam" at West Point)
  2. Robert E. Lee (2nd in Class of 1829; 0 demerits; married Mary Custis of Arlington House — Martha Washington's great-granddaughter)
  3. Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson (Class of 1846, 17th of 59; "Tom Fool" at West Point; his nickname "Stonewall" may have meant "stand like a stone wall" as an insult — he was holding his ground instead of attacking)
  4. James Longstreet (54th of 56 in Class of 1842; best man at Grant's 1848 wedding; opposed Pickett's Charge; became a Reconstruction Republican)
  5. George B. McClellan (2nd in Class of 1846; "Young Napoleon"; organized the Army of the Potomac; Lincoln fired him; ran as a Democrat against Lincoln in 1864)
  6. George Pickett (last in Class of 1846; Pickett's Charge named for his division, though Longstreet commanded the overall assault; blamed Lee afterward)
  7. Jefferson Davis (Class of 1828; Secretary of War under Pierce; President of the Confederacy; bitter feud with Joseph E. Johnston)
  8. Simon Bolivar Buckner (Class of 1844; friend of Grant's from West Point; surrendered Fort Donelson; marched in Grant's 1885 funeral)
  9. Winfield Scott Hancock (Union general at Gettysburg; Lewis Armistead was his best friend; shot off his horse during the battle; Armistead died calling for him)
  10. A.P. Hill (15th of 59 in Class of 1846; McClellan's roommate; both loved Ellen Marcy; Hill's Light Division arrived at Antietam after a 17-mile march to save Lee)

Section 3: Odd One Out

  1. d) Grant — Longstreet, Rosecrans, and Pope are all Class of 1842; Grant is Class of 1843.
  2. a) McClellan — The only Union general. Jackson, Pickett, and Longstreet all fought for the Confederacy.
  3. d) Custer — The only one who did NOT serve in the Mexican-American War. Custer was born in 1839 and graduated West Point in 1861, well after the war ended.
  4. d) Appomattox — Not a battle. Antietam, Gettysburg, and Fort Sumter were battles. Appomattox was a surrender negotiation.
  5. d) Lee — The only one who did NOT become a U.S. president. Grant was the 18th president; Longstreet and Sherman never held the office. (Trick: Longstreet and Sherman are red herrings — Grant is the actual answer.)

Section 4: Story Connections

  1. Love triangle. A.P. Hill and George McClellan both courted Ellen Marcy, who chose McClellan. This poisoned Hill's relationship with his former roommate and future commanding general, and may have contributed to Hill's poor performance during the Seven Days Battles.
  2. Post-war politics. Longstreet became a Republican — Grant's party — after the Civil War. This destroyed Longstreet's reputation in the South. Grant rewarded his loyalty with federal appointments (ambassador to Turkey, U.S. Marshal), but former Confederates never forgave him.
  3. Training ground. Nearly every major Civil War general — Grant, Lee, Jackson, Longstreet, McClellan, Sherman — served together in the Mexican-American War (1846–1848). They observed the same battles, learned the same lessons, and formed the relationships (friendships and feuds) that played out at Appomattox 17 years later.
  4. Enemies at Gettysburg. Lewis Armistead and Winfield Scott Hancock were best friends before the war. At Gettysburg, Armistead led a Confederate brigade in Pickett's Charge directly against Hancock's Union position. Hancock was shot from his horse; Armistead was mortally wounded, dying with a request that Hancock be told of his fate.
  5. Student vs. teacher. P.G.T. Beauregard (Class of 1838) had been an artillery student of Major Robert Anderson (Class of 1825) at West Point. When Beauregard bombarded Fort Sumter in April 1861, Anderson was the Union commander inside. The first shot of the Civil War was a West Pointer firing on his own instructor.

Section 5: True or False

  1. False. James Longstreet commanded the overall assault. George Pickett led only his division. The charge is named after Pickett's division, not the commanding general.
  2. False. His real name was Hiram Ulysses Grant. The "S." was added by a clerical error during his congressional appointment to West Point (the congressman thought his mother's maiden name "Simpson" was his middle name).
  3. True. Lee graduated second in the Class of 1829 with a perfect disciplinary record — zero demerits in four years.
  4. True. The Class of 1846 produced 22 generals (12 Union, 10 Confederate) — more than any West Point class in history.
  5. False. Longstreet strongly opposed the frontal assault on the Union center at Cemetery Ridge. He predicted it would fail, argued with Lee, and was ultimately overruled.
