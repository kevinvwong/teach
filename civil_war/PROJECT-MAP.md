# Project Map: West Point Drama

## Mission & Goals

A self-directed history course exploring the Civil War through the personal relationships forged (and fractured) at West Point. Character-driven, "reality TV" style. The goal is **Jeopardy readiness** — knowing the class years, roommate connections, and personal rivalries that shaped the war more than any battle tactic.

## Quick Reference: Module Summaries

### Module 0 — Numerical Anchors (Daily Drill)
- **File:** `lessons/0000-numerical-anchors.html`
- The 25 core facts to know cold — class years, class sizes, ranks, key counts, and relationship pairs ("couples"). Open before every session for a 2-minute drill.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Class of 1846: 59 graduates, 22 generals (37% rate)
  - Lee (1829, #2, 0 demerits) — only cadet to graduate without a single demerit
  - Beauregard (1838) — served 5 days as West Point superintendent in Jan 1861
  - Class of 1843: 56 graduates — Grant, Buckner, Sherman
  - Antietam: 23,000 casualties, bloodiest single day

### Module 1 — Prologue: The Cast of Characters
- **File:** `lessons/0001-cast-of-characters.html`
- Introduces the 5 main relationship arcs and the world of West Point — the Hudson River hilltop, demerit system, honor code, and four-company system that made 250 cadets know each other intimately.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - First Union officer killed was John H. Rodgers — shot by his own former artillery instructor
  - "Stonewall" may have been intended as an insult (not a compliment)
  - Pickett did NOT command Pickett's Charge (Longstreet commanded the assault)
  - Lee married George Washington's adopted granddaughter (Mary Custis)

### Module 2 — Grant's Web (Class of 1843)
- **File:** `lessons/0002-grants-web.html`
- Ulysses S. Grant's accidental social network — the reluctant cadet who hated West Point but made lifelong friends (Longstreet, Buckner, Sherman) who shaped the war.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Grant's real name: Hiram Ulysses Grant — the "U.S." was a congressman's mistake
  - Grant ranked 21st of 39 in the Class of 1843
  - Longstreet was best man at Grant's wedding
  - Buckner loaned Grant money in 1854; Grant let Buckner surrender at Fort Donelson in 1862
  - Grant rescued Sherman's career after Shiloh

### Module 3 — The Class of 1842
- **File:** `lessons/0003-class-of-1842.html`
- "The class the gods sent" — four corps commanders from 56 graduates: Longstreet (54/56), Rosecrans (3/56), Pope (17/56), and D.H. Hill (who married Stonewall Jackson's sister).
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Longstreet ranked 54th of 56 — almost last, one spot above expulsion
  - D.H. Hill married Stonewall Jackson's sister (became Jackson's brother-in-law)
  - Rosecrans briefly outranked Grant early in the war
  - Pope was so hated that his own officers celebrated his defeat at Second Bull Run
  - Longstreet and Grant: the roommate question (historians disagree if they shared a room)

### Module 4 — The Class of 1846
- **File:** `lessons/0004-class-of-1846.html`
- The most famous class in West Point history: 22 generals from 59 graduates. McClellan (#2), Jackson (#17), A.P. Hill (#15), Pickett (last — #59). The love triangle: McClellan, A.P. Hill, and Ellen Marcy.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - McClellan ranked 2nd — "the Young Napoleon," Lincoln called him "the slows"
  - Stonewall Jackson ranked 17th — the grinder who memorized every assignment
  - A.P. Hill ranked 15th — the speedster with the red battle shirt
  - Pickett ranked dead last (59th) — the class clown with the forever footnote
  - Ellen Marcy chose McClellan over A.P. Hill; Hill never forgave McClellan

### Module 5 — Lee's Generation (Classes of 1828–1829)
- **File:** `lessons/0005-lees-generation.html`
- The old guard: Lee (#2, 0 demerits, 1829), Joseph E. Johnston (1829 classmate), and Jefferson Davis (1828). The three-cornered feud that plagued Confederate leadership.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Lee is the only cadet known to graduate with zero demerits in four years
  - Lee married Mary Custis — great-granddaughter of Martha Washington
  - Lee served as Superintendent of West Point (1852–1855)
  - Lee was offered command of the Union army before choosing the Confederacy
  - Jefferson Davis (1828) was one year ahead of Lee; they feuded during the war

### Module 6 — Mexico: The Training Ground (1846–1848)
- **File:** `lessons/0006-mexico-training-ground.html`
- The Mexican-American War as a West Point reunion and battlefield classroom. Grant and Lee met for the first time — on the same side. Roughly 300 of 460 future Civil War generals served in Mexico together.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Grant and Lee's first meeting was in Mexico (1847); they wouldn't meet again until Appomattox (1865)
  - Grant saw Lee at Chapultepec and remembered it 18 years later
  - Winfield Scott's staff included Lee, McClellan, and Beauregard — all future Confederate/Union commanders
  - 300 of 460 future Civil War generals served in Mexico
  - Lee was Scott's engineer; Grant was a quartermaster

### Module 7 — The Fracture (1856–1861)
- **File:** `lessons/0007-the-fracture.html`
- How West Point tore apart in the run-up to war — the Emory Upton brawl, Beauregard's 5-day superintendency, Custer almost expelled, George Thomas disowned by his family for staying Union.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Beauregard served 5 days as West Point superintendent in January 1861
  - Custer graduated last in the Class of 1861 — almost expelled for visiting a tavern
  - George Thomas was disowned by his Virginia family for staying with the Union
  - Emory Upton fought Wade Hampton Gibbes in a brawl over abolition
  - The Class of 1861 had two graduations — one in April, one in June

### Module 8 — The First Shot (Fort Sumter, April 1861)
- **File:** `lessons/0008-first-shot.html`
- The Civil War begins as a West Point reunion: Beauregard (1838, former superintendent) fires on his own former artillery instructor, Robert Anderson (1825), at Fort Sumter.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Beauregard was Anderson's student at West Point — the student fired on the teacher
  - Beauregard graduated 2nd in the Class of 1838
  - Anderson graduated 15th of 37 in the Class of 1825
  - Anderson was from Kentucky (a border state that stayed in the Union)
  - The first cannonball symbolically announced the new rule of war: West Pointer vs. West Pointer

### Module 9 — Antietam (September 17, 1862)
- **File:** `lessons/0009-antietam.html`
- The bloodiest single day — 23,000 casualties — and the love triangle payoff: McClellan vs. A.P. Hill, with Hill's last-minute arrival saving Lee's army.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - 23,000 casualties in 12 hours — bloodiest single day in American history
  - McClellan found Lee's "Lost Order" (Special Order 191) wrapped around three cigars
  - A.P. Hill's Light Division marched 17 miles in 8 hours to save Lee at Antietam
  - Hill's arrival smashed into Burnside's flank at the critical moment
  - McClellan's caution cost him the chance to destroy Lee's army

### Module 10 — Gettysburg (July 1–3, 1863)
- **File:** `lessons/0010-gettysburg.html`
- The war's most personal battle: Armistead and Hancock — best friends separated by the war — face each other. Armistead touches Hancock's flag before falling. Custer vs. Rosser at East Cavalry Field.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Armistead did not graduate from West Point (expelled for breaking a plate over a cadet's head)
  - Armistead touched Hancock's flag as he fell — "No one on earth loved you more"
  - Pickett did NOT command Pickett's Charge (Longstreet commanded the assault)
  - Custer fought Rosser (his Class of '61 roommate) at East Cavalry Field
  - Hancock was wounded in the assault, survived the war, and later helped Armistead's widow

### Module 11 — Appomattox (April 9, 1865)
- **File:** `lessons/0011-appomattox.html`
- Grant and Lee's second meeting in 18 years — Grant accepts Lee's surrender with generous terms. Longstreet and Grant reconcile.
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Grant and Lee met only twice: Mexico (1847) and Appomattox (1865)
  - Grant wore a muddy private's coat; Lee wore his finest uniform with ceremonial sword
  - Grant's terms: officers keep sidearms, men keep horses (for spring planting)
  - Grant ordered 25,000 rations sent to Lee's starving army
  - Grant put Longstreet on a personal pardon list even though Longstreet refused to ask

### Module 12 — Epilogue: Reconciliation (1865–1885)
- **File:** `lessons/0012-epilogue.html`
- How the West Point network rebuilt the country — Longstreet becomes a Republican and is destroyed for it, Grant's presidency, Lee's final years, and the post-war reunions that proved "we were classmates before we were enemies."
- Key Jeopardy facts:
  - Longstreet became a Republican and supported Black suffrage — destroyed his reputation
  - Grant crushed the KKK through the Enforcement Acts
  - Lee became president of Washington College (later Washington and Lee)
  - Grant visited Lee at Washington College in 1867 — conversation never recorded
  - Lee died in 1870; Congress posthumously restored his citizenship in 1975

## Key Relationships Arc Index

1. **McClellan-Hill-Marcy Love Triangle** — Modules 1, 4, 9
   Roommates McClellan and A.P. Hill both love Ellen Marcy. She chooses McClellan. At Antietam, Hill's arrival at the critical moment smashes into McClellan's flank — the personal payoff of a love triangle fought on a battlefield.

2. **Grant-Longstreet Friendship** — Modules 1, 2, 3, 11, 12
   Grant and Longstreet are close friends before the war (Longstreet is best man at Grant's wedding). During the war they become enemies. After the war, Grant pardons Longstreet without being asked.

3. **Armistead-Hancock Tragedy** — Modules 1, 10
   Best friends serving in California before the war. At Gettysburg, Armistead leads Pickett's Charge against Hancock's line. Armistead is mortally wounded, touching Hancock's flag as he falls.

4. **Lee-Johnston-Davis Feud** — Modules 1, 5
   Lee (1829) and Johnston (1829) are classmates; Davis (1828) is one year ahead. The three-cornered feud poisoned Confederate command — Davis distrusted Johnston; Lee was caught between them.

5. **Custer-Rosser Cavalry Web** — Modules 1, 7, 10
   Custer and Rosser are classmates (Class of 1861) and friends. At Gettysburg, they face each other at East Cavalry Field. After the war, Custer invites Rosser to his wedding.
