Module 0 — Numerical Anchors

Daily Drill — 25 Core Facts for Jeopardy Readiness

How to use this page: Open before every study session. Cover the right column (the detail) with a sheet of paper. Read the number and key phrase. Try to recall the detail. 2 minutes, max. Do this daily and these numbers become automatic.

Class Years & Sizes

#1 West Point graduating classes 1825–1861 — the war's officer pool. These four decades produced the men who would command both sides.

#2 Class of 1828 — Jefferson Davis. One year ahead of Lee.

#3 Class of 1829 — Robert E. Lee (#2 in class, zero demerits). Joseph E. Johnston was his classmate.

#4 Class of 1838 — P.G.T. Beauregard. Future 5-day superintendent of West Point.

#5 Class of 1842 — Longstreet, D.H. Hill, Rosecrans, Pope. Known as "the class the gods sent."

#6 Class of 1843 — Grant (21st of 39), Buckner, Sherman.

#7 Class of 1846 — McClellan (#2), Jackson (#17), A.P. Hill (#15), Pickett (#59 / last). 22 generals from 59 graduates.

#8 Class of 1861 — George Custer. Last in his class, almost expelled.

Ranks & Positions

#9 Commanding General of the Union Army — Ulysses S. Grant, appointed 1864.

#10 Commanding General of the Confederate Army — Robert E. Lee, appointed 1865 (effectively commanded from 1862).

#11 Superintendent of West Point — P.G.T. Beauregard. Served 5 days in January 1861 — the shortest term in history.

#12 Secretary of War — Jefferson Davis (1853–1857, under President Pierce).

#13 President of the Confederate States — Jefferson Davis.

Key Counts

#14 22 generals from the Class of 1846 — 12 Union, 10 Confederate.

#15 0 demerits — Lee's record at West Point. Four years, not a single demerit.

#16 5 days — length of Beauregard's superintendency at West Point.

#17 ~23,000 casualties — Antietam, the bloodiest single day in American military history.

#18 59 graduates — Class of 1846, the most consequential class in West Point history.

#19 ~300 total West Pointers became Civil War generals — roughly 150 on each side.

Couples — Who Fought Whom

#20 Beauregard fired on Anderson

The first shot of the Civil War at Fort Sumter: Beauregard (Confederate, Class of 1838) ordered the bombardment against his own former artillery instructor, Major Robert Anderson (Union, Class of 1825). Instructor vs. student.

#21 McClellan vs. A.P. Hill

Roommates and best friends at West Point who both fell in love with Ellen Marcy. She chose McClellan. Hill never forgave him. At Antietam, Hill's division arrived at the critical moment and shattered McClellan's flank — the timing was personal. Roommates turned enemies.

#22 Armistead vs. Hancock

Best friends serving together in California before the war. Armistead went South; Hancock stayed North. At Gettysburg, Armistead led Pickett's Charge directly into Hancock's corps. He was shot and died with his hand on a Union cannon, surrounded by Hancock's men. His dying wish: to see his old friend one more time. Best friends on opposite sides.

#23 Grant vs. Lee

Two different classes (Grant '43, Lee '29), two different wars. Their only meeting before Appomattox was during the Mexican-American War. At Appomattox Courthouse, Grant and Lee sat at a single table to negotiate surrender — only their second meeting ever. Two classes, one table.

#24 Grant vs. Longstreet

Close friends before the war — Longstreet was best man at Grant's wedding. During the war they became enemies. After the war, Grant put Longstreet on a personal pardon list. Longstreet became the only Confederate general to publicly embrace Black suffrage and the Republican Party, destroying his reputation in the South — partly for Grant. Best man to enemy to reconciled.

#25 Custer vs. Rosser

Class of 1861. Close friends at West Point — Custer once saved Rosser from drowning. During the war they became cavalry rivals in the Shenandoah Valley. After the war, Rosser worked for the railroad and rehired Custer after he was fired from the same company. Classmates, rivals, then colleagues.
