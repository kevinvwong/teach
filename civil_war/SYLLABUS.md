# Syllabus: West Point Drama

## The Personal Relationships That Shaped the Civil War

---

**Mission:** To *feel* the Civil War through the personal relationships forged (and fractured) at West Point — friendships, rivalries, mentoring, and the tragedy of classmates turned enemies.

**Success looks like:**
- **Jeopardy ready** — can answer any clue about West Point connections between Civil War generals
- Can name the key West Point classes and their major figures (1846, 1843, 1829, etc.)
- Can tell the stories of specific friendships/rivalries (Grant-Longstreet, Armistead-Hancock, McClellan-Hill, Lee-Johnston)
- Can explain how pre-war relationships shaped battlefield decisions
- Can recommend one great book on the topic to another history buff

**Style:** Character-driven, "reality TV" approach. Think *The Crown* meets *Band of Brothers*, but with more love triangles and better nicknames.

**Prerequisites:** None — we start at the beginning.

---

## Jeopardy Core 25 — The Facts to Know Cold

These 25 numerical anchors appear in almost every module. Drill them until they're automatic.

### Class Years & Sizes
1. **West Point graduating classes 1825–1861** — the war's officer pool
2. **Class of 1828** — Jefferson Davis (one year ahead of Lee)
3. **Class of 1829** — Robert E. Lee (#2 in class, zero demerits)
4. **Class of 1838** — Beauregard
5. **Class of 1842** — Longstreet, D.H. Hill, Rosecrans, Pope ("the class the gods sent")
6. **Class of 1843** — Grant, Buckner, Sherman (56 graduates)
7. **Class of 1846** — McClellan, Jackson, A.P. Hill, Pickett (22 generals from 59 graduates)
8. **Class of 1861** — Custer (last in class, almost expelled)

### Ranks & Positions
9. **Commanding General of the Union Army** — Grant (1864)
10. **Commanding General of the Confederate Army** — Lee (1865)
11. **Superintendent of West Point** — Beauregard (5 days, Jan 1861)
12. **Secretary of War** — Jefferson Davis (1853–1857)
13. **President of the Confederate States** — Jefferson Davis

### Key Counts
14. **22 generals** from the Class of 1846
15. **0 demerits** — Lee's record at West Point
16. **5 days** — length of Beauregard's superintendency
17. **23,000 casualties** — Antietam, bloodiest single day
18. **56 graduates** — Class of 1843
19. **59 graduates** — Class of 1846

### Couples (Who Fought Whom)
20. **Beauregard fired on Anderson** — first shot, Fort Sumter
21. **McClellan vs. A.P. Hill** — roommates turned enemies (love triangle with Ellen Marcy)
22. **Armistead vs. Hancock** — best friends on opposite sides at Gettysburg
23. **Grant vs. Lee** — two classes, one table at Appomattox
24. **Grant vs. Longstreet** — roommates, then enemies, then reconciliation
25. **Custer vs. Rosser** — class of '61, cavalry rivals at Gettysburg

## Course Structure

13 modules. 12 numbered + Module 0 (the daily-drill reference). Each module is 1–3 short lessons. You can binge or space them — your call.

### Session 1: The Foundation

#### Module 0 — Numerical Anchors (Daily Drill)
*The 25 core facts to know cold. Return to this before every session.*

- Class years, ranks, counts, and couples (see Core 25 above)
- No lesson content — just the reference chart and a rapid-fire drill

#### Module 1 — Prologue: The Academy (1 lesson)
*What was West Point? The physical, social, and emotional world these teenage boys inhabited.*

- The Hudson River hilltop — isolation as a crucible
- Demerits, hazing, and the honor code
- The four-company system — how 250 cadets knew each other intimately
- Why West Point mattered more than any other factor in the war

---

### Session 2: The 1840s Classes

#### Module 2 — Grant's Web (Class of 1843) (2 lessons)
*The reluctant cadet who accidentally built the war's most valuable social network.*

- **Lesson 2a:** Grant hates West Point on sight (1839). U.S. Grant — middling student, brilliant horseman.
- **Lesson 2b:** The friendships that changed everything — Longstreet (roommate?), Buckner (loaned Grant money), Sherman (Grant rescued his career). 56 graduates, 7 future generals.

#### Module 3 — The Class of 1842 (1 lesson)
*"The class the gods sent." Four corps commanders in one graduating class.*

- Longstreet, D.H. Hill, Rosecrans, Pope — all graduated in the same year
- Longstreet and Grant: the roommate question (did they share a room?)
- D.H. Hill: the scholar-soldier who married Jackson's sister
- Rosecrans: the engineer who outran Grant (briefly)
- Pope: the braggart everyone hated — even his own side

---

### Session 3: The Star Classes

#### Module 4 — The Class of 1846 (3 lessons)
*The most famous class in West Point history. Twenty-two generals from 59 graduates.*

- **Lesson 4a:** The trivia matrix — rank × nickname × relationships. McClellan (#2), Jackson (#17), A.P. Hill (#15), Pickett (last — #59).
- **Lesson 4b:** The love triangle — McClellan, A.P. Hill, and Ellen Marcy. The defining personal drama of the class.
- **Lesson 4c:** Pickett — last in class, first in infamy. The class clown who got a forever footnote.

#### Module 5 — Lee's Generation (1825–1829) (2 lessons)
*The old guard — the men who were already legendary when Grant was still a nobody.*

- **Lesson 5a:** Lee — "the Marble Model." Second in his class (1829), zero demerits. The honor of his generation.
- **Lesson 5b:** The three-cornered feud — Lee (1829), Joseph E. Johnston (1829 classmate), and Jefferson Davis (1828 — one year ahead). Plus the Mary Custis / Washington connection: Lee married George Washington's adopted granddaughter.

---

### Session 4: The Crucible

#### Module 6 — Mexico: The Training Ground (1846–1848) (1 lesson)
*The war that taught them all how to fight — together. Bridge module.*

- Grant and Lee on the **same side**. Grant saw Lee at Chapultepec and remembered it.
- Who served together, who met who: the shared combat that forged bonds the Civil War would strain
- The mentorship network: older officers who became Confederate/Union leaders
- How Mexico shaped their later strategies — Lee the engineer, Grant the logistician

---

### Session 5: The Break

#### Module 7 — The Fracture (1856–1861) (1 lesson)
*West Point tears apart.*

- Beauregard's **5-day superintendency** (January 1861) — appointed and removed in less than a week
- Custer almost expelled — last in the Class of 1861
- George Thomas — Virginia-born Union general, disowned by his family
- Emory Upton brawl: the fight that showed cadets were already choosing sides
- The classes of 1861 — two graduations in one tragic spring

#### Module 8 — The First Shot (1861) (1 lesson)
*The war begins as a West Point reunion.*

- Beauregard (Class of 1838, former West Point superintendent) fires on Anderson (Class of 1825, his artillery instructor at West Point)
- West Pointer on West Pointer — the first cannonball announced the new rule of war

---

### Session 6: The Bloody Heart

#### Module 9 — Antietam: The Class Reunion (1862) (1 lesson)
*The bloodiest single day in American history — and for the Class of 1846, a reunion.*

- McClellan vs. A.P. Hill: the love triangle payoff. Hill's last-minute arrival saves Lee's army.
- Jackson vs. the Army of the Potomac: the grinder against the golden boy
- 23,000 casualties in one day. The class of '46 lost classmates they'd known since adolescence.

#### Module 10 — Gettysburg: The Heartbreak (1863) (1 lesson)
*The war's most famous battle, seen through its most personal tragedy.*

- Armistead & Hancock: the friendship destroyed in one afternoon. Armistead touches Hancock's flag before falling.
- Trick clue for Jeopardy: **Pickett didn't command Pickett's Charge.** Longstreet commanded the assault; Pickett led only his own division.
- Custer vs. Rosser: the cavalry boys of '61 meet again — East Cavalry Field

---

### Session 7: The End

#### Module 11 — Appomattox: The Final Reunion (1865) (1 lesson)
*The end of the war, the beginning of healing.*

- Grant and Lee: their **second meeting ever** (first was in Mexico, 1847)
- Grant's surrender terms: officers keep their sidearms, men keep their horses
- Grant and Longstreet: the post-war handshake
- Grant signs Longstreet's pardon — Longstreet never asked for one

#### Module 12 — Epilogue: Reconciliation (1 lesson)
*How the West Point network rebuilt the country.*

- Longstreet becomes a Republican — and is destroyed for it by former Confederate comrades
- Post-war reunions: "we were classmates before we were enemies"
- The strange legacy: what the West Point bond meant for America
